1. Predictive Sepsis Detection

An ML model that continuously analyzes EHR vitals, labs, and nursing notes to issue early-warning alerts for sepsis up to 6 hours before clinical deterioration—reducing ICU mortality by an estimated 18%.

Risk: Algorithmic bias across demographic groups; alert fatigue; clinician over-reliance
Pilot — 3 systems
👁️

2. AI-Assisted Radiology Triage

Computer vision model that reviews chest X-rays and CT scans to flag critical findings (pulmonary embolism, pneumothorax) and prioritize radiologist worklists, cutting read time by ~30%.

Risk: Performance disparities on underrepresented populations; liability if AI miss goes undetected
510(k) Pending
📊

3. Readmission Risk Stratification

Gradient-boosting model scoring patients at discharge for 30-day readmission probability. Used by care managers to allocate post-acute follow-up resources and reduce avoidable readmissions.

Risk: May encode historical socioeconomic inequities; risk of reduced care for low-score patients
Production — 85 hospitals
🎙️

4. Ambient Clinical Documentation

LLM-based system using ambient audio from patient-provider encounters to auto-draft structured SOAP notes, saving physicians 1.5–2 hours of documentation time per shift.

Risk: Patient consent; PHI privacy; model hallucination in clinical notes; HIPAA / 21st Century Cures

Medicare Health System — AI Ethics Statement
Medicare Health SystemOfficial Policy Document
Fairness & Equity

We commit to developing and deploying AI systems that perform equitably across all patient populations—regardless of race, ethnicity, sex, age, socioeconomic status, or disability. We will measure, monitor, and mitigate demographic performance gaps in every model we release.

Transparency & Explainability

Our AI systems will provide clinically actionable explanations for their outputs. We will document model design, training data, known limitations, and validation results in accessible Model Cards shared with all clinical partners.

Privacy & Data Security

We will collect only the minimum necessary patient data, apply rigorous de-identification standards, and implement technical and organizational safeguards that exceed HIPAA requirements. Patients will have clear rights regarding their data.

Safety & Clinical Reliability

No AI model will be deployed in clinical care without prospective validation on diverse, real-world populations. We will monitor performance continuously and will suspend or retrain any system that exhibits degradation in safety-critical metrics.

Accountability & Governance

Clear institutional accountability for every AI system is established before deployment. Our AEGO conducts mandatory reviews for all high-risk applications, and our Clinical Advisory Board provides independent clinical and ethical oversight.

Human Oversight & Control

AI at Medicare Health System augments—it never replaces—clinical judgment. Clinicians retain final decision-making authority in all patient-facing applications, and every system includes clear mechanisms for human override and escalation.

Adopted by the Medicare Health System Board of Directors · AI Ethics & Governance Office · Effective January 2026

Personal Reflection — Workshop 1
Medicare Health SystemResponsible AI Framework
1

Difficulty: Making Principles Concrete & Testable

The biggest challenge was avoiding vague language. It is easy to say "we value fairness," but Chapter 5 of Ethical Machines reminded me that a principle is only meaningful if it is testable. For our sepsis model, "fairness" must translate into specific demographic parity thresholds—that level of specificity feels hard to commit to upfront without full technical scoping.

2

Open Question: Whose Definition of Fairness?

Several fairness definitions (demographic parity, equalized odds, individual fairness) are mathematically incompatible. In healthcare, which should be optimized—equal false-positive rates across groups, or equal positive predictive value? The DoD RAI Strategy does not resolve this; it may require case-by-case deliberation with clinical and patient stakeholders.

3

Tension: Transparency vs. Intellectual Property Protection

How much should a Model Card disclose? Full transparency benefits clinical users, but proprietary training data and model architectures represent significant R&D investment. The DoD strategy (pp. 12–15) suggests "auditable by designated parties"—a middle ground between full openness and opacity that I found useful but underspecified.

4

Insight: Human Oversight Is a Design Problem, Not a Policy Problem

Research on automation bias shows humans often defer to confident-seeming AI even when it is wrong. Writing a meaningful human oversight principle requires designing workflows and interface affordances, not just drafting policy language. This gap between principle and practice was the most surprising insight from the readings.
